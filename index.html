Research interests

Brain decoding

My primary focus is brain decoding, which involves interpreting what a person is perceiving, thinking, or dreaming directly from their brain activity, with the ultimate goal of leveraging these technologies to build tools that help patients recover lost functions and overcome physical and communicative barriers.

In practice, this means applying machine learning algorithms to brain data such as fMRI, EEG and MEG. It fascinates me both as a way to gain a deeper understanding of how the human brain works and for its practical applications, such as brain-computer interfaces.

Papers that shaped my interest

Shen, Horikawa, Majima & Kamitani · PLoS Comput. Biol. 2019

Deep image reconstruction from human brain activity

This study fascinated me because it was the first I encountered that aimed to reconstruct images directly from brain activity (fMRI).

Kriegeskorte & Kievit · Trends Cogn. Sci. 2013

Representational geometry: integrating cognition, computation, and the brain

Kriegeskorte introduced and formalized representational similarity analysis (RSA), which is now widely used to compare different representational systems. I am particularly interested in applying RSA to compare representations in the brain and in artificial neural networks.
